Frequently Asked Questions

How do I select the area I want to keep?
Drag anywhere on the page preview to draw a rectangle. Everything inside it is kept and everything outside is trimmed away. Once drawn, drag inside the rectangle to move it, or drag any of the eight handles on its edges and corners to resize it precisely.
How do I add text on top of the PDF?
Draw your crop area first. A formatting toolbar appears just above it, then click anywhere inside the selection to place a text box. Type your text, adjust the font, size, bold, italic, underline, and colour from the toolbar, and click outside or press Ctrl+Enter to stamp it onto the page.
Can I move or edit text after placing it?
Yes. Drag any placed text to reposition it. Double-click it to reopen the editor and change the wording. A single click selects it so the toolbar controls apply to that item, letting you change its font or colour after the fact. Each text item keeps its own independent formatting.
Should I download as JPG or PDF?
Choose PDF when you want a document — if you have not added text, the crop is applied to the original file so the text stays selectable and the quality is untouched. Choose JPG when you need a picture to paste into a slide, a message, or a web page.
Does the crop apply to every page or just the one I see?
You draw the crop area on the page currently displayed, and that same region is applied to every page when you download as PDF. This works well for documents with a consistent layout, such as a scanned book where every page has the same margins.
Can I crop a scanned document to remove black scanner borders?
Yes — this is one of the most common uses. Drag a rectangle that covers only the paper itself and excludes the dark edges the scanner picked up. The result is a clean document with no black frame, which also prints far better and uses less toner.

Crop PDF Online — Free Visual PDF Cropping Tool

Most PDF croppers ask you to type margin measurements in millimetres and hope for the best. This one shows you the page and lets you draw the area you want to keep with your mouse. You see the result before you commit to it, you can nudge and resize the selection until it is exactly right, and you can add text on top before downloading as either a PDF or a JPG image.

Why Crop a PDF?

Scanner borders are the most common reason. When a page is smaller than the scanner glass, the surrounding area comes out as a black or grey frame that wastes toner when printed and looks unprofessional on screen. Cropping removes it completely.

Excessive margins are the second. Documents formatted for print often carry generous white space that serves no purpose on a screen — and on a phone or tablet it means the actual content is rendered uselessly small. Cropping the margins away makes the text noticeably larger and more readable on a mobile device.

Isolating a region is the third. Sometimes you only need one table, one chart, or one photograph out of a busy page. Draw a rectangle around it and download just that.

Drawing, Moving, and Resizing the Selection

Drag anywhere on the page to draw your first rectangle. From that point on the selection is fully editable: drag from inside it to slide the whole area to a new position, or grab any of the eight handles around its edge to resize. The dimensions in PDF points are shown live as you work, which is useful when you need to match a specific size.

Nothing is applied until you download, so you are free to adjust the selection as many times as you like. The Clear button removes it entirely and lets you start again.

Adding Text to Your PDF

Once a crop area exists, a formatting toolbar appears directly above it and you can click inside the selection to place a text box. Choose from six font families, set any size, apply bold, italic, or underline, and pick any colour. Each text item is independent, so one label can be large and red while another is small and black.

Placed text remains editable. Drag it to move, double-click to change the wording, or click once to select it and adjust its formatting from the toolbar. This makes the tool useful for annotating scanned forms, adding captions to figures, labelling diagrams, and marking up documents for review.

Choosing Between PDF and JPG

Download as PDF when you need a document. If you have not added any text, the crop is applied directly to the original file using its native page geometry, which means the text stays selectable and searchable and the quality is completely untouched. This is the option for anything that will be printed, archived, or read as a document.

Download as JPG when you need a picture — something to drop into a presentation slide, attach to a message, or embed in a web page. The image reproduces exactly what you see, including any text you added.

Who Uses This Tool

Students and researchers crop figures and tables out of papers to cite in their own work. Offices digitising paper strip scanner borders from thousands of archived pages. Anyone reading PDFs on a phone or e-reader trims margins so the text fills the screen properly. Designers extract artwork regions from proofs and layouts. Teams reviewing documents crop to the relevant section and add a text note before circulating it.

How to Crop a PDF

Upload your PDF by dragging it onto the box or clicking to browse. Use the page navigation to reach the page you want to work from. Drag to draw your crop area, then fine-tune it by moving the rectangle or dragging its handles. Add text if you need it. Click Download JPG or Download PDF and the result arrives immediately.

Your Privacy Is Protected

The page preview, the crop selection, and any text you add are all rendered locally in your browser — none of that leaves your machine. When you download, the file is processed in memory and discarded immediately afterwards. Nothing is written to disk and no copy is kept.
