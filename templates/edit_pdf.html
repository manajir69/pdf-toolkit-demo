Frequently Asked Questions

What can I edit in my PDF with this tool?
You can add new text anywhere on the page, choosing the font, size, and colour. This covers annotating, labelling, filling in forms that were never made fillable, and adding notes or corrections. It does not rewrite the text that is already in the document — see the next question for why.
Can I change the text that is already in the PDF?
Not directly, and this is true of PDFs generally rather than a limitation of this tool. A PDF stores text as positioned glyphs rather than editable paragraphs, so changing a word means reflowing everything around it. To edit existing content properly, convert with PDF to Word, edit in Word, then convert back with Word to PDF.
Can I fill in a form that is not fillable?
Yes, and this is one of the most common uses. Many official forms are distributed as flat scans with no interactive fields. Place a text box over each blank line, type your answer, and download — the result looks typed rather than handwritten and is far easier to read.
Will the text I add be selectable in the final PDF?
Yes. Text is added as real text rather than as a picture of text, so it stays selectable, searchable, and copyable in any PDF reader. It also stays sharp at any zoom level rather than becoming blurry.
Can I edit a password-protected PDF?
Yes. If your document is encrypted, a password prompt appears automatically after upload. Enter it once and editing proceeds normally. Our Unlock PDF tool can also remove the password permanently beforehand.
How do I permanently remove sensitive text rather than cover it?
Use Redact PDF for that. Drawing a black box over text in an editor only hides it visually — the words remain in the file and can be recovered by copying the text out. Redaction removes the underlying content properly.

What "Editing a PDF" Actually Means

PDFs were designed to look identical everywhere, which is exactly what makes them awkward to edit. A word processor stores text as flowing paragraphs; a PDF stores it as individual characters placed at fixed coordinates. There is no paragraph to reflow, which is why changing one word in the middle of a sentence is a genuinely difficult operation rather than a simple one.

What is straightforward is adding text. Placing new content at a chosen position does not disturb anything already on the page, and that covers the overwhelming majority of what people actually need: filling blanks, adding notes, labelling, correcting, and signing off.

Filling In Forms That Are Not Fillable

This is the single most common use of the tool. Government departments, schools, clinics, and older corporate systems still distribute forms as flat PDFs — often scans of printed paper — with no interactive fields at all. The conventional route is to print the form, write on it by hand, scan it back, and email the result, which takes twenty minutes and produces a document nobody can read.

Instead, open the form here, place a text box over each blank line, and type your answer. The finished document looks typed rather than handwritten, stays perfectly legible, and never needs to touch a printer. For anything requiring a signature, Sign PDF adds a signature image to complete it.

How to Edit a PDF

Upload your PDF by dragging it onto the box or clicking to browse. Click where you want text to appear and a box opens at that point. Type your content and set the font, size, and colour. Repeat for as many text items as the document needs — each keeps its own independent formatting. When everything is in place, download the edited PDF.

Added Text Stays Real Text

Text you add is written into the document as genuine text rather than as a picture of text. That means it stays selectable, searchable, and copyable in any PDF reader, and it remains perfectly sharp at any zoom level instead of turning into a blurry bitmap when someone zooms in to read it. It also keeps the file size small, since text occupies a tiny fraction of what an image of the same content would.

When You Need to Change Existing Text

For genuine revision of content already in the document, the reliable route is a round trip through a word processor: PDF to Word converts the PDF into an editable document, you make your changes in Word where reflowing text is natural, then Word to PDF converts it back. Complex layouts do not always survive perfectly, but for text-led documents this works well and is far less frustrating than fighting the PDF format directly.

Editing Is Not Redaction

This distinction matters and catches people out. Drawing a black rectangle over sensitive text hides it visually but leaves the words in the file, where anyone can recover them by selecting and copying the region. If you need information genuinely removed rather than merely covered, use Redact PDF, which deletes the underlying content rather than painting over it.

Your Privacy Is Protected

The page preview and your text placement are rendered locally in your browser. When you download, the file is processed in memory and discarded immediately afterwards. Nothing is written to disk, nothing is logged, and no copy is kept — which matters when you are filling forms with personal details.
