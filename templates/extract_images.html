Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Extract Images and PDF to Image?
Extract Images pulls out the original embedded photos and graphics at their true resolution, exactly as they were inserted into the document. PDF to Image renders each whole page as a picture, including the text and layout. Use Extract Images when you want the pictures themselves, and PDF to Image when you want a snapshot of the page.
Will the extracted images lose quality?
No. Images are pulled straight from the PDF at the resolution they were embedded, so nothing is re-compressed or downscaled. If you choose PNG the result is lossless; JPG is saved at 92% quality for a smaller file size.
Why did the tool skip some images?
By default images smaller than 50 pixels are skipped, because those are usually bullets, dividers, or decorative icons rather than real pictures. Lower the minimum size setting to 1 if you want absolutely every image including the tiny ones.
Can I extract images from a password-protected PDF?
Yes. If your PDF is encrypted, a password box appears automatically after upload. Enter the password and extraction continues normally. You can also remove the password permanently first with our Unlock PDF tool.
How are the images named inside the ZIP?
Each file is named by its source page and position, like page3_img1.png. This makes it easy to trace every picture back to where it appeared in the original document.
Are my files stored on your server?
No. Your PDF is processed entirely in memory and the ZIP is streamed straight back to your browser. Nothing is written to disk or retained after your download completes.

Extracted Images Keep Their Original Quality

This is the part that matters most. A screenshot of a PDF page gives you a flattened picture at whatever resolution your screen happens to be. Extraction is different: it recovers the image data exactly as the author embedded it. If a magazine layout contains a 4000-pixel-wide photograph, you get that full 4000-pixel photograph back — not a blurry approximation of it. Nothing is re-encoded, resampled, or squeezed on the way out.

Extract Images vs. PDF to Image — Which One Do You Need?

These two tools sound alike but do genuinely different jobs, and picking the wrong one is the most common mistake people make.

Extract Images gives you the pictures on their own. Text, headings, page borders, and background colour are all left behind. This is what you want when you need to reuse a photograph from a brochure, recover the figures from a research paper, or pull product shots out of a supplier catalogue.

PDF to Image takes a photograph of the entire page — text, layout, images, everything — and saves it as one flat picture per page. Reach for that when you need a visual preview, a thumbnail, or a page you can post somewhere that does not accept PDFs.

Understanding the Minimum Size Filter

Most PDFs are full of images you do not actually want. A bullet point, a hairline divider, a corner ornament, a one-pixel spacer — technically these are all embedded images, and a naive extractor would dump hundreds of them into your ZIP alongside the three photographs you were actually after. The minimum size filter solves this by ignoring anything below a chosen pixel dimension. Leave it at 50 for normal documents. Raise it to 300 or more if you only want large, print-quality photographs. Drop it to 1 if you are auditing a file and genuinely need to see every single image object it contains.

Choosing Between PNG and JPG

PNG is lossless. Nothing is thrown away, transparency is preserved, and screenshots, logos, line art, and diagrams stay perfectly crisp. Choose it whenever quality matters more than file size, or when the image has a transparent background you need to keep. JPG uses compression that discards a small amount of detail invisible to the eye, producing files that are often several times smaller. Choose it for photographs, or when you are extracting a large number of images and want a ZIP that is quick to download and share.
