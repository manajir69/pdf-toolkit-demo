Frequently Asked Questions

Which image formats can I convert?
JPG, PNG, and WEBP are all supported, and you can mix formats within a single conversion. Photographs from a phone, screenshots, and scanned pages can all go into the same document together.
Can I control the order the images appear in?
Yes, and you should always check it. After uploading, each image appears as a card you can drag into position. The PDF follows the order shown on screen — file names rarely sort the way you actually want the pages to read, so a quick visual check is worth the few seconds.
Does each image become its own page?
Yes. Every image becomes one page in the resulting document, sized to fit the image. This is what makes the tool useful for turning a set of photographed pages back into a readable multi-page document.
Will my images lose quality?
No. Images are embedded at their original resolution with no additional compression applied. A photograph that was sharp before conversion is equally sharp in the PDF. If the resulting file is larger than you need, run it through Compress PDF afterwards.
Can I turn phone photos of documents into a PDF?
Yes — this is one of the most common uses. Photograph each page, upload them all, drag them into the right order, and download a single PDF. It turns a phone into a serviceable document scanner without any additional software.
Is there a limit on how many images I can add?
There is no fixed limit. Very large batches of very high-resolution photographs will take longer to process and produce a correspondingly large file, but ordinary document-sized batches convert quickly.

Why Convert Images to PDF?

One file instead of many. Twenty separate photographs of a contract is an awkward thing to send and an easy thing to lose pages from. One twenty-page PDF is a document — it stays together, prints in order, and files cleanly.

Order is preserved permanently. Images in a folder sort by file name, which frequently has nothing to do with the sequence the content should read in. Once they are pages in a PDF, the order is fixed and cannot be scrambled by whatever system opens them next.

Documents should look like documents. Sending a landlord, a school, or a government office a folder of JPGs looks improvised. Sending a single PDF looks like you knew what you were doing.

Turning Your Phone Into a Scanner

This is the most common use of the tool by a wide margin. Photograph each page of a document with your phone, upload the pictures here, drag them into the right sequence, and download a proper multi-page PDF. No scanner needed and no app to install.

A few things noticeably improve the result. Shoot in even, indirect light — a window works better than a ceiling lamp directly overhead, which casts your own shadow across the page. Hold the camera parallel to the paper rather than at an angle, so the page edges stay rectangular. Fill the frame with the page. And check each shot before moving on; retaking a photo takes five seconds, while discovering page seven is unreadable after you have posted the document is a much larger problem.

Who Uses This Tool

Anyone submitting documents online to a portal that requires a single PDF rather than loose images. Students photograph handwritten notes and lecture whiteboards to compile into study documents. Freelancers and small businesses turn photographed receipts into one expense claim. Tenants and applicants combine photographed identity documents, payslips, and certificates into one submission. Anyone archiving paper without access to a scanner.

How to Convert Images to PDF

Upload your images by dragging them onto the box or clicking to browse — you can select many at once, and mix JPG, PNG, and WEBP freely. Each appears as a card you can drag into position. Arrange them until the order matches how the document should read, then convert and download.

Getting the order right is the step worth attention. Phone cameras name files sequentially by capture time, which usually works, but any photo you retook will be out of position. The visual arrangement lets you catch that immediately.

Quality Is Preserved

Images are embedded at their original resolution with no additional compression. A sharp photograph goes into the PDF equally sharp, and a high-resolution scan stays high-resolution. The consequence is that a document built from twenty phone photographs can be quite large — modern phone cameras produce several megabytes per shot. If the file needs to be smaller for emailing, Compress PDF reduces it substantially, since photographic data compresses very well.

After Converting

If some photographs came out rotated, Rotate PDF corrects them. If the pages have distracting backgrounds around the edges — a desk surface visible around the paper — Crop PDF trims them away. To combine your new PDF with existing documents, use Merge PDF.

Your Privacy Is Protected

Your images are processed in memory and discarded the moment your download completes. Nothing is written to disk, nothing is logged, and no copy is retained — which matters when the pictures are of identity documents, bank statements, or medical records.
