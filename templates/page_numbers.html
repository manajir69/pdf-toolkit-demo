Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I place the page numbers?
Choose a position at the top or bottom of the page, aligned left, centre, or right. Bottom centre is the conventional choice for most documents; bottom outer edge suits anything that will be bound, since it stays visible when pages are turned.
Can I start numbering from a page other than the first?
This matters for documents with a cover or title page that should not carry a number. Where a starting page can be set, begin from the first page of actual content so the numbering matches how the document reads.
Will page numbers overlap my existing content?
Choose a position within the page margin, where documents leave space by design. Check the result on a page with content near the edge, since documents with unusually narrow margins occasionally need a different position.
Why add page numbers if my document already has them?
Merged documents are the usual reason. Combining three reports that each numbered themselves from one gives you a file containing three page ones. Applying continuous numbering across the merged document restores a sequence that actually works.
Are page numbers added as real text?
Yes. They are written as text rather than as images, so they stay sharp at any zoom level, remain searchable, and add almost nothing to the file size.
Can I add numbers to a password-protected PDF?
Yes. A password prompt appears automatically after upload if the file is encrypted. Enter it once and numbering proceeds normally. Our Unlock PDF tool can also remove the password first.

Why Page Numbers Matter More Than They Seem

An unnumbered document cannot be discussed precisely. Every reference becomes approximate — "the section after the budget table", "somewhere near the end" — and in a meeting where several people are looking at printed copies, finding the same place takes a minute of shuffling every single time.

They also expose loss. Printed documents get dropped and reassembled, and a numbered document immediately reveals a missing sheet. An unnumbered one hides the gap indefinitely, which for a contract or a legal filing is a genuine problem.

And they signal completeness. Numbered pages read as a finished document; unnumbered ones read as a stack of printouts.

The Most Common Reason: Merged Documents

This is where the tool earns its place. When you combine several PDFs with Merge PDF, each source document brings its own numbering with it. The result is a single file containing three separate page ones, three page twos, and no way to reference anything unambiguously.

Applying continuous numbering across the merged document fixes it in one step. The sequence runs properly from start to finish and the document behaves like the single document it now is.

Choosing a Position

Bottom centre is the default convention for good reason — it is where readers look, it works for both bound and loose documents, and it rarely collides with content.

Bottom outer edge is the traditional choice for anything bound, because it remains visible as pages are turned and is the first thing the eye finds when flipping through.

Top right suits documents that will be filed in a folder or clipped together, where the bottom edge may be obscured.

Whichever you choose, keep it in the margin area. Documents leave that space deliberately, and numbers placed there will not collide with body text.

Handling Cover Pages

A title page carrying "1" looks wrong to anyone who reads documents regularly. Convention numbers the first page of actual content as page one, leaving covers, title pages, and blank separators unnumbered. Where a starting page can be configured, begin from the content rather than from the cover — it is a small detail that noticeably distinguishes a carefully prepared document from a quickly assembled one.
