Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between this and Extract Images?
This tool renders each whole page as a picture — text, layout, graphics, everything flattened into one image per page. Extract Images instead pulls out the original embedded photographs on their own, without the surrounding text. Use this for page snapshots; use that for the pictures themselves.
Should I choose JPG or PNG?
PNG is lossless and keeps text edges perfectly crisp, which makes it the better choice for pages that are mostly text or line art. JPG produces much smaller files and is well suited to pages dominated by photographs. When in doubt on a text-heavy document, choose PNG.
How are multiple pages delivered?
Each page becomes its own image file, and when there is more than one they are packaged into a single ZIP so you get everything in one download. Files are named by page number so the order is never in doubt.
Will the images be high enough quality to print?
Screen use and web publishing are well covered. For professional printing, a rendered page image is generally not the right source — printers want the vector PDF, which stays sharp at any size. Send the PDF itself for print work rather than an image of it.
Can I convert just one page instead of all of them?
Extract the page you want first with Extract Pages, then convert that single-page PDF here. This avoids generating and downloading images for pages you have no use for.
Can I convert a password-protected PDF?
Yes. A password prompt appears automatically after upload if the document is encrypted. Enter it once and conversion proceeds normally. You can also strip the password permanently first with Unlock PDF.

Why Convert a PDF to Images?

Because so many places do not accept PDFs. Social media posts, chat messages, forum uploads, product listings, and most web content management systems take images happily and PDFs reluctantly or not at all. Converting sidesteps the problem entirely.

Because a picture always displays. An image renders in every browser, email client, and messaging app without a plugin, a download prompt, or a viewer that may or may not be installed. For a document that simply needs to be seen rather than read closely, that reliability matters.

Because you need a thumbnail or preview. Websites, catalogues, and document management systems routinely need a visual representation of the first page.

Because you want to prevent easy editing. An image of a page cannot have its text selected and copied straight out, which is occasionally exactly what you want.

PDF to Image vs. Extract Images

These two operations sound similar and are frequently confused, so it is worth being clear.

This tool photographs the entire page. Everything you see — body text, headings, tables, background colour, and any embedded pictures — is flattened into a single image per page. The result looks exactly like the page.

Extract Images reaches inside the PDF and pulls out only the embedded photographs and graphics, at their original resolution, without any of the surrounding text. The result is the pictures on their own.

If you want a snapshot of the page, you are in the right place. If you want the photograph that appeared on the page, use the other tool.

Choosing Between JPG and PNG

PNG is lossless, meaning nothing is discarded during encoding. Text edges stay perfectly crisp, thin lines stay clean, and there are none of the faint halos that compression can introduce around lettering. Choose it for pages that are mostly text, line art, diagrams, or screenshots — which is most documents.

JPG uses compression that discards detail the eye does not readily notice, producing files that are often several times smaller. It handles photographic content very well. Choose it for pages dominated by images, or when you are converting a long document and want a ZIP that is quick to download and share.
