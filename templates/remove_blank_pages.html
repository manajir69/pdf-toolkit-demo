Frequently Asked Questions

How does the tool know a page is blank?
Each page goes through three separate checks. First it looks for any text characters. Then it looks for embedded images. If both come back empty, the page is rendered and the tool measures what percentage of the pixels are non-white. Only pages that fail all three checks are marked blank — which is why a page holding just a single stray character or a small logo is never removed by accident.
What does the sensitivity setting do?
Sensitivity sets how much ink a page may contain and still count as blank. At the default of 0.2% a page with faint scanner speckle is still treated as empty. Raise it toward 1% for noisy scans with dust, shadows, or edge marks. Lower it to 0.05% when you only want to remove pages that are perfectly, absolutely white.
Should I scan first or remove straight away?
Scanning first is always safer. It lists exactly which page numbers will be deleted without changing your file, so you can confirm the result matches what you expect before committing. If anything looks wrong, adjust the sensitivity slider and scan again — it costs nothing to check.
Why are the backs of my double-sided scans blank?
Duplex scanners capture both sides of every sheet regardless of whether the back was printed on. A twenty-page single-sided document scanned in duplex mode becomes a forty-page PDF where every second page is empty. This tool removes all of those in one pass.
Will removing pages affect the quality of the rest?
No. The pages you keep are copied across untouched with their original resolution, fonts, and embedded images intact. Only the empty page objects are dropped, so your file size goes down while quality stays exactly the same.
Can I remove blank pages from a password-protected PDF?
Yes. A password prompt appears automatically after upload if the document is encrypted. Enter it and processing continues as normal. Our Unlock PDF tool can also strip the password permanently beforehand.

Blank pages creep into PDFs almost every time a document passes through a scanner. They pad out the file, break the flow when someone reads it on screen, and waste paper and toner when the document goes to print. Where Blank Pages Come From

Duplex scanning is the biggest culprit by far. A double-sided scanner captures the back of every sheet whether or not anything was printed on it, so a thirty-page single-sided report arrives as a sixty-page PDF with every alternate page empty. Chapter breaks in books and manuals deliberately leave the reverse of a section divider blank so the next chapter opens on a right-hand page — useful in print, pointless on screen. Merged documents collect the trailing blank pages of every file that went into them. Fax and multi-function printer output often adds an empty cover or trailer page to each transmission. Word and Excel exports frequently tack on a stray final page created by a paragraph mark or print range that spilled past the last row of content.

How the Detection Actually Works

A page can look blank to you and still contain hidden data, so a single test is not enough. Every page runs through three checks in sequence. The first extracts the text layer and asks whether a single character exists. The second lists the embedded image objects. Only when both come back completely empty does the third check run: the page is rendered to a grayscale bitmap and the tool counts what fraction of pixels are darker than pure white. A page is marked blank only when it fails all three. This layered approach is what stops the tool from deleting a page that holds nothing but a page number, a thin footer line, or a small watermark.

Getting the Sensitivity Right

Real scans are rarely pure white. Paper texture, dust on the glass, the shadow along a book spine, and bleed-through from the reverse side all leave faint marks that a naive detector would count as content. The sensitivity slider tells the tool how much of that noise to forgive.

The default of 0.2% handles most documents correctly. Push it toward 0.5–1% when working with old paper, low-quality scans, or pages with visible speckle and edge shadows. Pull it down to 0.05% when your PDF was generated digitally rather than scanned, and you want to be certain that only genuinely empty pages are touched.
