Frequently Asked Questions

Can I rotate just one page instead of all of them?
Yes. Every page appears as a thumbnail with its own rotate button, so you can fix a single sideways scan while leaving the rest untouched. This is the normal case with scanned documents, where usually only a few sheets fed in the wrong way.
What rotation angles are available?
Each click turns the page 90° clockwise. Click twice for 180° to flip an upside-down page, and three times for 270°, which is the same as 90° counter-clockwise. The current angle is shown under each thumbnail so you always know where you are.
Does rotating affect text quality or searchability?
No. Rotation is stored as a page property that the PDF reader applies when displaying the page, not by re-rendering the content as an image. Text stays perfectly sharp, fully selectable, and searchable exactly as before.
Will the rotation stick when I open the file elsewhere?
Yes. The rotation is written into the document itself, so every standards-compliant PDF reader — Acrobat, Preview, Chrome, Edge, and mobile apps — displays the pages the way you set them. It is not a temporary view setting.
Can I rotate a password-protected PDF?
Yes. If your file is encrypted, a password prompt appears automatically after upload. Enter it once and the pages render as normal. To strip the password permanently instead, use our Unlock PDF tool first.
Can I rotate and reorder pages at the same time?
Not on this page — this tool focuses on rotation alone. If you need to rotate, reorder, and delete pages together in one pass, use Organize PDF, which combines all three in a single visual editor with one upload and one download.

Why Pages End Up Rotated

Sheet feeders pick up paper in whatever orientation it was stacked, so a bundle that was not squared before scanning produces a mix of correct and rotated pages. Landscape content — spreadsheets, wide tables, technical drawings — is often scanned in portrait mode and ends up needing a quarter turn. Photographed documents inherit the orientation the camera was held in, and phone cameras frequently record this inconsistently. Merged files combine documents that each had their own orientation convention, producing a single PDF where some sections read one way and others another.

Rotation Is Lossless

This is worth understanding because it explains why the operation is completely safe. A PDF page carries a rotation attribute — a number the reader consults before drawing the page. Setting it to 90 tells every reader to display that page turned a quarter turn. The page content itself is never touched, re-rendered, or re-compressed.

The practical result is that text stays perfectly crisp and fully selectable, vector graphics remain sharp at any zoom, photographs keep their exact original resolution, and the file size does not change at all. You can rotate a page a dozen times and the twelfth result is bit-for-bit as good as the first.

Anyone who photographs documents with a phone corrects the inevitable orientation mistakes afterwards.

How to Rotate PDF Pages

The thumbnail turns as you click, so you can see exactly what the finished document will look like. When everything reads correctly, download the corrected file.

Because the preview reflects the real result, there is no guesswork and no need to download, check, and try again.

Getting the Orientation Right for Print

Orientation matters more on paper than on screen. A reader can tilt their head or rotate the view; a printed sheet is fixed. Correcting rotation before printing means the document comes out readable in the intended binding direction, which is essential for anything being bound, filed, or distributed as hard copy. It is worth a final check of every page before committing a long document to print.
