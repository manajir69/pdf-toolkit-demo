Frequently Asked Questions

Can I control where the watermark appears?
Yes. You can set the text, its position on the page, the size, and the transparency. Diagonal placement across the centre is the most common because it is hard to crop away, while a corner position is less intrusive when the document still needs to be read closely.
Will the watermark appear on every page?
Yes. The text is applied to all pages of the document, which is the point — a marking that only appears on the first page is trivially defeated by removing that page.
Does the watermark make the text underneath hard to read?
Not if the transparency is set sensibly. A light watermark is clearly visible as a marking while leaving the document comfortably readable. Increase the opacity when deterrence matters more than legibility, and reduce it when the document must be read closely.
Can someone remove the watermark?
A watermark is a deterrent, not a security control. Someone determined and technically capable can work around one. It signals status and discourages casual misuse — for genuine protection use Protect PDF, and to remove sensitive content entirely use Redact PDF.
What text should I use?
Whatever states the document's status unambiguously: DRAFT, CONFIDENTIAL, SAMPLE, COPY, NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION, or your organisation's name. Adding a date or recipient name is useful when you want to trace where a leaked copy originated.
Can I watermark a password-protected PDF?
Yes. A password prompt appears automatically after upload if the file is encrypted. Enter it once and the watermark is applied normally. Our Unlock PDF tool can also remove the password first.

Why Watermark a Document?

To prevent a draft being mistaken for a final version. This is the most valuable use and the least dramatic. Documents circulate, get saved, and resurface months later with no memory of their status. A page stamped DRAFT cannot be mistaken for approved copy no matter how far it travels or how long it sits in someone's downloads folder.

To mark ownership. A company name across a proposal or portfolio makes the source clear wherever the file ends up.

To label samples and previews. Sharing a portion of paid work — a report extract, a design mockup, a course chapter — marked SAMPLE lets you demonstrate value while discouraging the preview being used as the product.

To discourage casual redistribution. A conspicuous CONFIDENTIAL stamp makes forwarding feel like a deliberate act rather than an oversight.

Choosing Position and Transparency

These two settings determine whether the watermark helps or gets in the way.

Diagonal across the centre is the classic arrangement for a reason. It covers the body of the page, cannot be cropped off without destroying the content, and is impossible to overlook. Use it when the marking matters more than comfortable reading — drafts, confidential material, and samples.

Corner or edge placement is far less intrusive and suits documents that need to be read carefully while still carrying an identifier. A company name in the footer of every page is a good example.

Transparency is the balance point. Too light and the marking is easy to ignore; too heavy and the document becomes tiring to read. A moderate setting where the watermark is unmistakably present but the text beneath is comfortably legible works for most purposes.

What Text Works Best

Short and unambiguous. DRAFT for anything unfinished. CONFIDENTIAL for restricted material. SAMPLE or PREVIEW for partial content. COPY for duplicates of a signed original. NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION when the boundary needs stating explicitly. Your organisation name for ownership.

Adding a date or the recipient's name is worth considering when you are sending the same document to several parties — if a copy surfaces where it should not, the watermark tells you which distribution it came from.

A Watermark Is a Deterrent, Not Security

Being honest about this matters. A watermark discourages casual misuse and makes document status unmistakable. It does not stop a determined and technically capable person from removing it, and it does nothing to prevent the content being read.

For actual protection, use the right tool for the job. Protect PDF encrypts the file so it cannot be opened without a password. Redact PDF permanently removes sensitive content rather than marking the page around it. A watermark complements these; it does not replace them.
