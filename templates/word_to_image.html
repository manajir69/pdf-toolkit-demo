Frequently Asked Questions

What do I get back?
One image per page of the document, packaged into a ZIP when there is more than one. Files are named by page number so the sequence is always clear.
Should I choose JPG or PNG?
PNG is lossless and keeps text edges perfectly crisp, which suits documents since they are mostly text. JPG produces smaller files and handles photographic content well. For a typical Word document, PNG gives the better-looking result.
Why convert a document to images rather than PDF?
Because images go where PDFs cannot — social media posts, chat messages, forum uploads, and web content systems that only accept pictures. An image also displays inline everywhere without a viewer or a download prompt.
Will the text be selectable in the images?
No. An image is a picture of the page, so the text becomes pixels rather than characters. That is sometimes exactly the point — it makes casual copying harder — but if you need selectable text, convert to PDF instead with Word to PDF.
Do fonts and formatting look correct?
Yes. The page is rendered as it appears with the correct fonts, spacing, images, and layout, then captured as a picture. What you see in the image is what the page looks like.
Are my files stored on your server?
No. Your document is processed in memory and the images are streamed straight back to your browser. Nothing is written to disk, nothing is logged, and no copy is retained.

Why Convert a Document to Images?

Because images go where documents cannot. Social media platforms, messaging apps, forums, marketplace listings, and most web content systems accept pictures readily and document files reluctantly or not at all. Converting sidesteps the restriction completely.

Because an image always displays. A picture renders inline in every browser, email client, and chat app — no download prompt, no plugin, no "you need an app to open this". For content that simply needs to be seen, that reliability is worth a great deal.

Because it discourages easy copying. Text in an image cannot be selected and pasted straight out. This is not real protection, but it does prevent casual lifting of your wording.

Because you need a preview or thumbnail. Websites, portfolios, and listings often need a visual representation of a document's first page.

Choosing Between JPG and PNG

PNG is lossless — nothing is discarded during encoding. Text edges stay perfectly crisp, thin lines stay clean, and there are none of the faint compression halos that can appear around lettering. Since Word documents are mostly text, PNG is usually the better choice.

JPG compresses more aggressively, producing files that are often several times smaller. It handles photographic content very well but can soften the edges of small text. Choose it when file size matters more than absolute crispness, or when your pages are dominated by images.

Text Becomes Pixels

This is the fundamental trade-off and worth being clear about. Once a page is an image, the text is no longer text. It cannot be selected, copied, or searched, and screen readers cannot read it aloud.

For a social media post or a chat message that is fine, and the resistance to copying may even be a benefit. For anything that needs to remain accessible, searchable, or usable as a document, convert to PDF instead with Word to PDF, which keeps the text real while fixing the layout.

Accessibility Is Worth a Thought

An image of text is invisible to screen readers, which affects anyone using assistive technology. If your content is going somewhere public, consider providing the text in an accessible form alongside the image — most social platforms support alternative text descriptions for exactly this reason.
