Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I send a PDF instead of a Word file?
Because a PDF looks the same everywhere. A .docx re-flows according to the fonts and page settings on the recipient's machine, so your carefully aligned document can arrive with broken pagination and substituted typefaces. A PDF is fixed — what you see is exactly what they see.
Will my fonts and images be preserved?
Yes. Fonts are embedded into the PDF so the document renders correctly even on a computer that does not have them installed. Images are carried across at their original resolution, and page size, margins, and orientation are all maintained.
What Word formats are supported?
Modern .docx files convert reliably. The older .doc format from pre-2007 Word is far less consistent — if you have one, opening it in Word or LibreOffice and re-saving as .docx first will give a noticeably better result.
Do headers, footers, and page numbers convert correctly?
Yes. Headers, footers, page numbering, and section breaks all carry across as they appear in Word. Tables of contents convert as text, though the clickable navigation may need rebuilding depending on how it was created.
Can I convert several Word files into one PDF?
Convert each document individually, then combine the results with Merge PDF. This gives you full control over the order of the sections, which is usually better than a bulk conversion that guesses at sequence.
Are my files stored on your server?
No. Your document is processed in memory and the PDF is streamed straight back to your browser. Nothing is written to disk, nothing is logged, and no copy is retained after your download completes.

Why Convert Word to PDF?

Layout stability is the main reason. A Word document does not carry its appearance with it — it re-flows based on the fonts installed and the default page settings on whichever computer opens it. Send a carefully formatted proposal as .docx and it can arrive with different line breaks, a table split across two pages, and a substituted typeface that ruins the design. A PDF has all of that baked in and cannot shift.

Universal access is the second. Not everyone has Word, and mobile devices in particular handle .docx inconsistently. Every phone, tablet, and computer opens a PDF without hesitation.

Professionalism is the third. Sending a PDF signals a finished document. Sending an editable .docx implies a draft, and invites the recipient to change it — sometimes without telling you.

What Carries Across

Nearly everything, and reliably so. Fonts are embedded into the PDF, which means the document renders correctly even on a system that has never had those typefaces installed — this alone solves the most common cause of documents "looking wrong" when shared. Images are preserved at their original resolution. Tables, lists, and indentation retain their structure. Headers, footers, page numbers, and section breaks all appear as they do in Word. Page size, margins, and orientation — including mixed portrait and landscape sections — are maintained.

Hyperlinks generally remain clickable. Automatically generated tables of contents convert visually, though the internal navigation depends on how the original was built.

A Note on Old .doc Files

The modern .docx format is an open, well-documented standard and converts predictably. The older binary .doc format from Word 2003 and earlier is far messier, and conversions from it are correspondingly less reliable. If you have a .doc file, open it in Word or LibreOffice and save it as .docx first. The extra step takes a moment and produces a markedly better PDF.

Converting Several Documents
